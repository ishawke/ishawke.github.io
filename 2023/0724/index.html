Reflections on July

July 24, 2023 life views:

July is almost over, and I’ve temporarily escaped the grind of college life. As graduation approached, I wrote a reflection —back when I was still in the middle of my graduate school entrance exam interviews. Now that everything is over, I’ve been accepted into graduate school and have already left campus.

After returning to campus in April, I spent every day relaxing in my dorm room—though“relaxing”was really just an excuse to indulge myself. Even though my final project wasn’t quite finished, I knew it was almost done, so I’d just lie in bed playing with my phone. In the evenings, I’d usually buy a bottle of soda and start eating and drinking to my heart’s content. But when I thought about all the hardships I’d endured over the past few years, drinking soda felt like a form of comfort. Sometimes, when I thought about the final project I was working on, I’d open my coding software and write some code. No one was really worried about failing the thesis—and there was indeed no need to worry, since we all knew what the school was like. Without a doubt, those few months were the most carefree time of our college lives.

So, during the Labor Day holiday in early May, a few of us guys wanted to get away and discussed where to spend our vacation. After some back-and-forth with my roommates, the three of us decided to head to Qingdao for a few days. It was our first time at the beach. As three kids from inland areas, our long-held dream of seeing the ocean finally came true. From Rizhao in Shandong to Qingdao, we stayed in a small hotel in a county town; after all, at 430 per person, that’s what we could afford. We headed to Qingdao the next day. I took a long-distance bus—the ride took several hours, and the bus was filled with college students just like us. We figured it was probably everyone’s first time seeing the ocean, and the beauty of the sea was truly breathtaking and captivating, especially on the Qingdao Jiaozhou Bay Bridge, where the sight of the city surrounded by the sea left a lasting impression on me. As we entered the bustling city center, Qingdao gradually came into view. However, that day wasn’t exactly pleasant—we got up at 6 a.m. to set off and didn’t arrive until 10 a.m. We got off the bus right by the seaside, near the Zhanqiao Pier, a popular Instagrammable spot. I got tired after just a few steps; the aftermath of my indulgence significantly slowed my pace as I explored Qingdao. Afterward, we visited Xiao Qingdao, the Underwater World, the First Seawater Bathing Beach, Badaguan, and Zhongshan Park. By the time we reached Zhongshan Park, it was already 6:00 p.m. Finding a place for lunch had been a challenge, so I settled for a noodle shop next to the beach—I’d originally planned to have a seafood buffet, but the place was already packed at noon. While walking, I tripped over a round stone pillar and stumbled—a classic Chinese slip-up, if you will—which was one of the major disappointments of this trip. To us, Qingdao felt enormous; there was simply too much to see in one day. With so many attractions, we called it a day at Zhongshan Park. My friends and I decided to head back, feeling a bit regretful—it’s true that you can’t really see much in just one day.

In early June, it was time to submit the first draft of our thesis. I’d already finished my graduation project, so I didn’t feel any pressure. We went through the defense—it was just a formality; basically, you just showed up and passed. Professors wouldn’t intentionally make students delay graduation. Two weeks later, the university held the graduation ceremony. We received our diplomas and certificates, and that was that. After three years of the pandemic, it’s been tough to even find a job—it’s driven everyone crazy—but we’ll keep moving forward. The Class of ‘23 really couldn’t have had it any worse. There were six of us in our dorm; four took the graduate school entrance exam, and I was the only one who got in. Those who didn’t make it are preparing to try again next year. Of the other two, one signed a tripartite employment agreement and started working, while the other just coasted through graduation and went home to try to land a job. And just like that, our college dorm life came to an end, and we all went our separate ways—north, south, east, west, and center.

In late July, I received my admission letter from graduate school, but I wasn’t happy about it. I don’t like campus life—it’s stifling and dull. Most importantly, I’m broke. Financial pressures have been pushing me to start working and earning money as soon as possible, and I’m really anxious. Plus, as a student, I can’t help but face all kinds of judgmental stares. I want to stand on my own two feet, think for myself, and make my own choices. I want a stable environment where I can truly relax—and I want to see the world out there! I can’t let my life go to waste any longer!
