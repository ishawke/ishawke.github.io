Reflections on My Final Days in College

February 8, 2023 life 本文总阅读量次

When I first decided to take the graduate school entrance exam, I didn’t really know why I had to do it. Deep down, I thought to myself: If I get into graduate school, I’ll have a better advantage in the future. My reasoning was very simple. During the nearly year-long preparation process, I felt physically and mentally exhausted—it was practically torture. My daily routine was a monotonous cycle between home, the library, and my dorm; I spent most of my time sitting on a bench reading and studying, relying only on listening to music and fantasizing about a so-called“bright future”to keep myself going.

I finally stopped studying right before the exam and completely gave up on myself. I barely touched a book during the exam period. There were special circumstances then—the school had ordered us to quarantine. Originally, everyone was supposed to go home to quarantine and then return for the exam, but everyone in my dorm got infected with COVID-19, and on the day of the exam, I had a fever and a headache. It didn’t matter anymore. With only two or three days left, I had no heart to study; I just wanted to go home as soon as possible. As I sat on the train heading home, watching the trees rush by outside the window, I realized just how incredibly difficult this past year had been.

It was just like the 2022 Qatar World Cup: Messi led Argentina to the World Cup final, while I was frantically working through math problems. Each of us was striving desperately toward our goals, giving it our all. In the end, Argentina won, but I didn’t know if I had won. Is life really just a competition or a game?

Looking back on my four years of college, I entered university half-awake, bringing my past ignorance and naivety with me. I floundered this way and that but never found that “passion” the big shots always talked about. Maybe the campus was too small, or perhaps it was because my abilities and resources were limited. I spent most of my college years in a daze. During that time, I did try to break free from mediocrity—I took the CET-4 and CET-6 exams along with everyone else—but what’s the point of passing them? Now that I’m a junior, I’ve fallen into a different kind of confusion: Should I apply for graduate school? There are all kinds of opinions online—some say applying for grad school is a turning point in life; others say it’s a way to avoid graduation and entering the workforce; still others say it’s for a better future self, or that it’s a way to improve oneself. I don’t understand what exactly I’m supposed to be improving—my blood pressure and weight? Or my susceptibility to being deceived? If it’s just for the degree, forget it. To be honest, I do admire those classmates who worked hard to get into graduate school. Online, it’s mostly about prestigious universities. Whenever I see videos of students from non-985 and non-211 schools pulling off an underdog victory against 985 and 211 institutions, I feel even more lost. I don’t know what it’s all for—I’m just following others’ paths and plodding along step by step, without ever listening to what my heart truly wants. So this past year has been incredibly painful—carrying books back and forth, not returning to the dorm until past 10 p.m., always running away from something. I’m not sure what it is. Since entering college, I suddenly felt as if I’d been set free—much more independent and unrestricted—and that’s exactly why I’ve been so lost. No one ever introduced me to college life, and no one told us what we were supposed to do in college. There are plenty of online guides, though, covering every aspect of life, all laid out clearly and in detail.

Graduation is just around the corner, and I’m still just as at a loss as ever. Between preparing for my final project, potential graduate school entrance exams, and job hunting, I can’t possibly handle everything. That’s just how life is—there are gains and losses—and I’m ready to face whatever the outcome may be. Maybe I don’t really care about the final result; I suppose I just like the feeling of reaping a small reward after putting in the effort. It can be pretty disheartening when you put in the work but don’t get anything in return.

If I get into graduate school as I hope, will that make up for the slacking off and confusion of these past few years in college? I’ve fallen back into a state of “confusion.” I’m in graduate school now, poring over academic papers and writing code every day. My advisor might only reach out once a week. Once I graduate, I won’t know how to face life ahead, how to fit into this unfamiliar society, or how to survive in this world. It’s as if the university has built a thick wall to shield us inside—it seems I’ve merely switched schools only to continue feeling lost. When I was filling out my college application, I chose computer science out of mere curiosity. I hadn’t had much opportunity to interact with computers before college, and it wasn’t until I was hit with a barrage of tedious courses that I truly got my start in the field. After that, my knowledge remained largely theoretical; the most practical use I got out of it was playing video games with classmates—so I suppose I also got my start in computer games during my undergraduate years.

Perhaps it’s time that pushes us forward, until we come to understand ourselves and begin to accept reality: I’m just a“pre-programmed person,”destined to receive an education and then enter the workforce—otherwise, how could I be called a programmer? Being a programmer isn’t so bad. Now that I’m nearing the end of my university’s journey, I don’t have to worry about not having enough money for food, shelter, and daily expenses. My mind is much more at ease and peaceful—perhaps I’m no longer lost. Of course, buying a house might be a problem, but it’s better than being unemployed and living off my parents. At least I have a stable income to support my family. There will be plenty of worries down the road, so I won’t dwell on daydreams anymore. In any case, I’ll just let things take their natural course. Parents want their children to make something of themselves, but this is just who I am. Do they even know the pressure we’re under these days? Young people are working their hearts out, all just to buy a place to call home in the city of their choice.

Although my exam scores aren’t out yet, the final thesis and corporate spring recruitment have already begun. It’s time to leave this confusion behind and set out on a path that brings me peace of mind. These past few years of the pandemic have been really tough—it’s been so hard!
